About the project

Safety-bounded and Interpretable ML for Networks

When applied to communication networks, traditional approaches for control and decision-making require a comprehensive knowledge of system and user behaviours, which can become impractical as networks grow in scale and complexity. Data-driven AI approaches do not have this drawback, but offer no safety bounds and are difficult to interpret.

Today, computer networks are facing important architectural transformations. Software Defined Networking (SDN) introduced the decoupling of control and data planes; network virtualisation, data plane programmability, in-network processing with AI accelerators and programmable pipelines enable more agility and intelligence inside networks. Most of this transformation is supported by the long-term vision of self-driving networks, where automation and intelligence are two essential ingredients.

Network and service providers are facing increasing complexity, an ever-growing variety of traffic and applications, and users demanding better Quality of Experience (QoE) — while OPEX/CAPEX must stay contained. Networks should largely self-manage: routing, resource allocation, QoE and traffic engineering all require new algorithms for decision-making and control.

Networking knowledge-based approaches and data-driven approaches should work in synergy: models guide and control the learning of data-driven ML, which in turn learns about new situations from new data.

Control and decision-making algorithms are critical for the operation of networks, hence the solutions should be safety bounded and interpretable. This is a scientific barrier that needs to be lifted, as network operators are reluctant to use ML in production networks because of their critical and sensitive nature — outages and performance degradations can be very costly.

Objectives

Three main scientific objectives

01

Hierarchical architecture

A machine-learning architecture with a global AI running at the central controller level and local AIs running at the edge device level, for decision-making in partially as well as fully observable and controllable environments. The global AI controls, configures and installs policies on the local AIs.

02

Partially observable environments

New safety-bounded and interpretable algorithms for intelligent path selection, automatic queueing and scheduling in partially observable and controllable environments. Key use case: SD-WANs, where edge devices at customer premises collaboratively operate an overlay on top of an uncontrollable, only partially observable core network.

03

Fully observable environments

New safety-bounded and interpretable algorithms for flow scheduling, software-defined routing and traffic engineering, targeting data centers and private WANs connecting multiple data centers, performing closed-loop actions to improve network utilisation and optimise QoS and QoE.

Technical approach

Reinforcement learning & graph neural networks

RLReinforcement Learning

RL agents learn an optimal policy by interacting with the environment. Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) extends RL with neural networks to enhance the learning capabilities of intelligent agents — for partially observable environments and for traffic engineering in SD-WAN. DRL has also been applied inside data center networks.

GNNGraph Neural Networks

Initial ML-based routing approaches that ignore the graph topology of networks may not outperform domain-knowledge models. GNNs capture key properties from the topology and network conditions, and learnt models generalise over new topologies. SAFE combines GNNs with optimisation to cope with the combinatorial nature of routing and traffic engineering, and with DRL to handle their dynamic nature.
